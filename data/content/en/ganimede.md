## Science

Ganymede is the largest moon in the Solar System: with a diameter of 5,268 km it is larger than the planet Mercury, although it has about half its mass. It is the only known moon with its own magnetic field, generated by a molten iron core, which produces small auroras.

Beneath the surface, divided between very old dark regions and bright grooved regions, there is probably a salty ocean sandwiched between layers of ice. Hubble's observations suggested it in 2015: the auroras wobbled less than expected, as if a conducting ocean were damping Jupiter's magnetic field.

## History

Galileo discovered it on 7 January 1610. The name, proposed by Simon Marius, comes from the cupbearer of the gods. The European probe JUICE, launched in 2023, will enter orbit around Ganymede in 2034: it will be the first probe to orbit a moon other than ours.

## Fun facts

- If it orbited the Sun instead of Jupiter, Ganymede would be classified as a planet.
- In 2021 the Juno probe flew over its surface at about 1,000 km, the closest pass in twenty years.
- Ganymede can be seen with binoculars: it is one of the four "little stars" lined up next to Jupiter.
