## Science

S2 (called S0-2 by the American team) is the most famous star at the center of the Galaxy: a young, massive star of about 14 solar masses that orbits the black hole Sagittarius A* in just 16 years, on a very elongated orbit.

At its closest point it passes about 120 times the Earth-Sun distance from the black hole (four times Neptune's distance from the Sun) and races at more than 7,500 km/s, 2.5% of the speed of light. At its farthest point it moves out to almost 2,000 times the Earth-Sun distance.

## History

From its orbit, followed for decades by the teams of Reinhard Genzel and Andrea Ghez, the mass of the black hole was derived: about 4.3 million solar masses in a tiny space. In 2018, at its closest approach, the GRAVITY instrument measured the gravitational redshift of its light; in 2020 the slow rotation of its orbit (Schwarzschild precession). These are two predictions of general relativity, verified for the first time around a supermassive black hole.

## Fun facts

- Its next closest approach will be in 2034: speed up time in Atlante Celeste to watch it whiz around the black hole.
- From S2 the black hole would appear as a black disk smaller than the Sun seen from Earth, surrounded by a sky full of very bright stars.
