## Science

The Orion Nebula (M42) is the region of massive star formation closest to the Earth, about 1,300 light years away. It is a cloud of gas lit up and ionized by the very young stars it contains: at its center is the Trapezium, a group of four hot, massive stars born less than a couple of million years ago, whose ultraviolet light makes the gas glow.

What we see is only the "surface" facing us of a much larger molecular cloud, the Orion complex, where stars keep being born. The Hubble Space Telescope photographed hundreds of protoplanetary disks there, the disks of gas and dust from which planets form: it is like seeing the Solar System as it was 4.6 billion years ago.

## History

To the naked eye it appears as the middle star of Orion's "sword", below the belt. The first telescopic description dates back to 1610, by the Frenchman Nicolas-Claude Fabri de Peiresc. Charles Messier included it in his catalog in 1769 as number 42. In 1880 the American Henry Draper took the first astronomical photograph of a nebula with it.

## Fun facts

- On a dark night, far from cities, it can be seen with the naked eye as a fuzzy smudge; with binoculars its fan shape already appears.
- The pink color in photographs is the red light of hydrogen (the H-alpha line); to the eye, in a telescope, it looks greenish because of the emission of ionized oxygen and because the eye is more sensitive to green.
- In a few hundred thousand years the Trapezium stars will have dispersed the gas, and a cluster of stars will remain in place of the nebula, like the Pleiades.
