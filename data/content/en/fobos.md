## Science

Phobos is the larger and closer of the two moons of Mars: an irregular block of about 27 × 22 × 18 km orbiting just 6,000 km above the surface, closer to its planet than any other moon in the Solar System. It goes around Mars in 7 hours and 39 minutes, faster than Mars rotates.

It is dominated by the Stickney crater, 9 km across, and furrowed by long parallel grooves. Martian tides are slowly drawing it closer to the planet, by almost two meters per century: in 30 to 50 million years Phobos will be shattered and might form a ring around Mars. Its origin (captured asteroid or fragment of an impact on Mars) is still debated.

## History

It was discovered by Asaph Hall in August 1877 at the U.S. Naval Observatory in Washington, a few days after Deimos. Hall was about to give up; it was his wife, Angeline Stickney, who persuaded him to keep going: the largest crater bears her name. The Japanese MMX mission is designed to collect samples from Phobos and bring them back to Earth.

## Fun facts

- From Mars, Phobos rises in the west and sets in the east, about twice a day.
- In *Gulliver's Travels* (1726) Jonathan Swift gave Mars two small moons, 150 years before their discovery: a famous coincidence.
- Phobos and Deimos mean "fear" and "dread": they are the sons of the god of war.
- Phobos is so small and so close that from the surface of Mars, near the poles, it can never be seen: it stays below the horizon.
