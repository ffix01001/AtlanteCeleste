## Science

Eris is one of the most distant known dwarf planets: its orbit, tilted by 44° with respect to the planets', takes it between about 38 and 97 times the Earth–Sun distance, and a full orbit takes about 560 years. Today it is near the farthest point of its orbit.

It is almost exactly as large as Pluto (about 2,330 km) but about a quarter more massive, and therefore rockier. Its surface is one of the whitest in the Solar System: it reflects about 96% of the light, probably because its atmosphere of nitrogen and methane is frozen on the ground like frost.

## History

Eris was spotted in January 2005 by Mike Brown, Chad Trujillo and David Rabinowitz, in images taken in 2003 at Palomar Observatory. More massive than Pluto, it forced astronomers to make a choice: either the Solar System had ten planets (and soon many more), or a new definition was needed. In 2006 the IAU chose the second path, creating the category of dwarf planets.

## Fun facts

- The name is that of the Greek goddess of discord: it could not have been more fitting, given the dispute it caused.
- Before the official name the discoverers called it "Xena", after the TV series. Its moon is called Dysnomia, the goddess of lawlessness: a hidden tribute to the actress Lucy Lawless.
- In 2023 it was found that Eris rotates in sync with Dysnomia: its "day" lasts as long as the moon's orbit, about 16 days.
