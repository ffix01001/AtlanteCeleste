## Science

The Lagoon Nebula (M8) is a large cloud of hydrogen in Sagittarius, about 4,000 light years from us and more than a hundred light years across. It is a "star factory": the ultraviolet light of the very young, very hot stars born inside it makes the gas glow with the typical red of hydrogen.

At its center lies the open cluster NGC 6530, made of stars a few million years old. Near the heart is the "Hourglass Nebula", shaped by the wind and the radiation of the star Herschel 36, an O-type star tens of times more massive than the Sun.

## History

It was noticed before 1654 by the Sicilian astronomer Giovanni Battista Hodierna. Guillaume Le Gentil described it as a nebula in 1747 and Charles Messier included it in his catalog in 1764 as number 8.

## Fun facts

- The name comes from the dark lane of dust that crosses it, like a channel in a lagoon.
- It is one of the very few nebulae that can be seen with the naked eye: from Italy, on summer nights, it appears as a small bright smudge low in the south, above the "teapot" of Sagittarius.
- In the sky it lies close to the Trifid Nebula (M20): the two fit in the same field of view of binoculars.
