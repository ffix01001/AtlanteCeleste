## Science

The Andromeda Galaxy (M31) is the large spiral galaxy closest to the Milky Way, about 2.5 million light years away. It is larger than our Galaxy and contains about a trillion stars; together with the Milky Way and the Triangulum Galaxy it is one of the three large members of the Local Group.

We see it steeply inclined, almost edge-on (about 77°): that is why it looks like an elongated ellipse. It is approaching us at about 110 km/s; the most recent simulations, based on the motions measured by Hubble and Gaia, give about a one-in-two chance that the two galaxies will collide and merge within the next 10 billion years.

## History

It is the most distant object that can easily be seen with the naked eye (under perfect skies some people can also make out the Triangulum Galaxy, slightly farther away). The Persian astronomer al-Sufi described it in 964 as a "small cloud". For centuries it was considered a nebula inside our Galaxy.

In October 1923 Edwin Hubble, at the Mount Wilson telescope, recognized a Cepheid variable star in Andromeda: on the photographic plate he corrected the label "N" (nova) to "VAR!" in pen. From Henrietta Leavitt's period-luminosity relation he derived an enormous distance, far outside the Milky Way: it was the proof that the "spiral nebulae" are galaxies like ours, and that the Universe is immensely larger than was thought.

## Fun facts

- On a dark autumn night, from Italy, it can be seen with the naked eye as a small elongated smudge in the constellation Andromeda. In reality its disk spans more than three degrees in the sky: six times the diameter of the full Moon, but the outer parts are too faint for the eye.
- The light we see tonight set out when the first human beings of the genus *Homo* lived on Earth.
- In Atlante Celeste you have to move very far away to see it: it is at the limit of the zoom, about 765 kiloparsecs away.
