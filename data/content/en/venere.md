## Science

In size and mass Venus is almost Earth's twin, but it is a hell. Its atmosphere, 96.5% carbon dioxide, presses on the ground at 92 bar, the pressure we would find 900 meters deep in the sea. The greenhouse effect brings the surface to about 464 °C, enough to melt lead: it is the hottest planet in the Solar System. The sky is covered by clouds of sulfuric acid.

Venus rotates backwards compared with almost all the other planets, and extremely slowly: one rotation lasts 243 days, longer than its year (225 days). The atmosphere, on the other hand, races: the upper clouds circle the planet in about 4 days, a phenomenon called super-rotation.

The surface is volcanic and relatively young. In 2023, re-examining the radar images of the Magellan probe, researchers found a volcanic vent that had changed shape within a few months: a clue that volcanism is still active.

## History

In 1610 Galileo saw that Venus shows phases, like the Moon: decisive proof that it goes around the Sun. The rare transits of Venus across the solar disk (1761 and 1769) mobilized expeditions all over the world to measure the Earth–Sun distance; among those who observed one, in 1769 in Tahiti, was Captain James Cook.

Venus was the first planet reached by space exploration: Mariner 2 flew past it in 1962. The Soviet Union landed the Venera probes there: Venera 7 (1970) was the first object to transmit from the surface of another planet, Venera 13 (1982) sent the first color photos and survived 127 minutes before being destroyed by the heat. The future missions DAVINCI and VERITAS (NASA) and EnVision (ESA) will go back to study it.

## Fun facts

- On Venus the Sun would rise in the west and set in the east, very slowly: about 117 Earth days pass from one sunrise to the next.
- After the Moon it is the brightest natural object in the night sky. It is the "morning star" and the "evening star" of the ancients.
- Between 50 and 55 km of altitude, pressure and temperature are similar to those on Earth: that is why there are studies of future "floating" bases in the atmosphere.
- In 2020 the possible presence of phosphine in the clouds was announced, a gas that on Earth is linked to life. The result is still hotly debated.
- The Soviet Veneras were designed like submarines in reverse: they had to withstand crushing pressure instead of vacuum.
