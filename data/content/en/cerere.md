## Science

Ceres is the largest body in the main asteroid belt, about 940 km across, and on its own it contains about a quarter of the mass of the whole belt. Since 2006 it has been classified as a dwarf planet: its gravity has made it almost spherical.

The Dawn probe (NASA), in orbit from 2015 to 2018, revealed a world rich in ice and salts. The very white spots in the Occator crater are deposits of sodium carbonate left by brines that rose from an underground reservoir; Ahuna Mons, an isolated mountain about 4 km high, is probably a cryovolcano, a volcano that erupted icy mud instead of lava.

## History

On 1 January 1801 the astronomer Giuseppe Piazzi, from Valtellina, noticed from the Palermo Observatory a star moving among the others. He called it "Ceres Ferdinandea", in honor of the patron goddess of Sicily and of King Ferdinand of Bourbon. It was the first asteroid ever discovered.

After a few weeks Ceres was lost in the glare of the Sun. The young mathematician Carl Friedrich Gauss invented a new method to calculate its orbit from a few observations, and thanks to his prediction Ceres was found again a year later. That method underlies techniques still used throughout science today.

## Fun facts

- The chemical element cerium, discovered in 1803, is named after Ceres.
- For half a century Ceres was considered a planet in every respect, until the discoveries of other asteroids "demoted" it.
- Dawn was the first probe to enter orbit around two different bodies of the Solar System: first Vesta, then Ceres.
- A layer of liquid brine might still exist beneath its crust today.
