## Science

Amalthea is a small irregular moon, about 250 km long, that orbits very close to Jupiter in less than 12 hours. It is one of the reddest objects in the Solar System. In 2002 the Galileo probe, passing by, measured its density: it is lower than that of ice, a sign that Amalthea is a porous heap of fragments held together by gravity. The dust kicked up by impacts on its surface feeds one of Jupiter's faint rings.

## History

It was discovered on 9 September 1892 by Edward Emerson Barnard at Lick Observatory. It was the fifth moon of Jupiter to be found, almost three centuries after Galileo's, and the last satellite of a planet discovered by looking directly through the eyepiece, without photography.

## Fun facts

- The name is that of the goat (or the nymph) that nursed the infant Zeus.
- From Amalthea, Jupiter would fill a huge part of the sky.
