## Science

The Carina Nebula (NGC 3372) is one of the largest and brightest star-forming regions of the Milky Way, about 7,500 light years away in the southern constellation Carina. It is four times larger than the Orion Nebula and contains some of the most massive stars known.

The most famous is Eta Carinae, a binary system whose main star probably has more than 90 solar masses. It is unstable: in the "Great Eruption" of the 1840s it ejected about ten solar masses of gas, which today form the Homunculus Nebula, a double bubble around the star. Sooner or later Eta Carinae will explode as a supernova.

## History

The nebula was discovered by Nicolas-Louis de Lacaille in 1751-52, during his expedition to the Cape of Good Hope. In 1843 Eta Carinae became for a while the second brightest star in the night sky after Sirius, then faded until it disappeared from naked-eye view, only to slowly brighten again in recent decades.

## Fun facts

- It is not visible from Italy: with a declination of about −60° it always stays below the horizon. It can be seen from the southern hemisphere, where it is one of the finest sights in the sky.
- The 2022 images from the James Webb telescope show the "Cosmic Cliffs", the edge of a cavity in the nebula sculpted by young stars.
