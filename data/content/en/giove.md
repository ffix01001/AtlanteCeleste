## Science

Jupiter is the giant of the Solar System: its mass is two and a half times that of all the other planets put together, and about 1,300 Earths would fit inside it. It is made mostly of hydrogen and helium, like a failed star, and has no solid surface: going down, the gas becomes liquid and then, under enormous pressures, metallic hydrogen that conducts electricity. Data from the Juno probe point to a "diluted" core, fuzzy and not as compact as was thought.

It spins faster than any other planet, in less than 10 hours, and this rotation stretches the clouds into colored bands and visibly flattens it at the poles. The Great Red Spot is an anticyclone wider than the Earth, observed continuously at least since 1830, which has been shrinking in recent decades. Jupiter's magnetosphere is the largest structure in the Solar System: if it were visible, it would look bigger than the full Moon in our sky.

## History

In January 1610, from Padua, Galileo discovered the four large satellites that now bear his name and dedicated them to the Medici family as the "Medicean Planets". It was the proof that not everything in the sky goes around the Earth. Giovanni Domenico Cassini, born in Perinaldo in Liguria, measured its rotation and flattening and described a large spot in 1665. In 1676 the Dane Ole Rømer, timing the eclipses of the moon Io, realized that light does not travel instantaneously and estimated its speed for the first time.

Pioneer 10 was the first probe to fly past it (1973), followed by Pioneer 11 and the two Voyagers, which discovered the rings and the volcanoes of Io. The Galileo probe studied it from orbit from 1995 to 2003, also dropping a capsule into its atmosphere. In 1994 the whole world followed the fragments of comet Shoemaker-Levy 9 crashing into Jupiter. Today Juno is in a polar orbit, while JUICE and Europa Clipper are on their way to its moons.

## Fun facts

- It has the shortest day in the Solar System: less than 10 hours.
- Jupiter and the Sun revolve around a common barycenter that lies just outside the surface of the Sun.
- To ignite like a star, that is to start fusing hydrogen, it would need to be about 80 times more massive.
- Its polar auroras are hundreds of times more energetic than those on Earth and shine mostly in ultraviolet light and X-rays.
- Juno discovered groups of cyclones at the poles arranged in almost regular geometric figures: a pentagon and an octagon around a central cyclone.
- Jupiter has rings, but they are faint and dark: Voyager 1 discovered them in 1979.
