## Science

New Horizons is the probe that showed us Pluto. After its flyby on 14 July 2015 it continued into the Kuiper Belt, and on 1 January 2019 it flew past Arrokoth, the most distant object ever explored up close. Today it keeps studying the environment at the edges of the heliosphere and photographing distant bodies from viewpoints impossible from Earth.

## History

It was launched on 19 January 2006, when Pluto was still considered a planet, with the highest launch speed ever reached by a spacecraft: it passed the Moon's orbit in about 9 hours. In 2007 it used Jupiter's gravity to speed up further.

## Fun facts

- Part of the ashes of Clyde Tombaugh, the discoverer of Pluto, and two American commemorative coins travel on board.
- The data from the Pluto flyby, transmitted at a few thousand bits per second, took more than a year to all reach the Earth.
