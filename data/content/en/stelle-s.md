## Science

The "S stars" are the stars closest to the black hole at the center of the Milky Way, Sagittarius A*. A few dozen are known: they circle the black hole on orbits of a few decades, tilted in every direction, like a swarm of bees around a flower. Many are young, massive B-type stars, which is a mystery: near the black hole tidal forces should prevent stars from being born. Perhaps they formed farther out and were pushed there by close encounters.

The orbits in Atlante Celeste are those measured by Reinhard Genzel's team (Gillessen and collaborators, 2017) after 25 years of observations with ESO's telescopes.

## Fun facts

- They are observed in the infrared: in visible light the dust between us and the galactic center lets through only one part in a thousand billion.
- Some, such as S14 and S175, have orbits so elongated that at their closest approach they skim the black hole at a few tens of times the Earth-Sun distance.
