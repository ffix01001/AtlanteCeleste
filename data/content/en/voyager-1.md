## Science

Voyager 1 is the human-made object farthest from the Earth. After flying past Jupiter (March 1979) and Saturn (November 1980), with a close pass by Titan, it continued outwards at about 17 km/s. On 25 August 2012, at about 121 times the Earth–Sun distance, it crossed the heliopause: it entered interstellar space, where the Sun's wind gives way to the gas of the galaxy.

It runs on radioisotope generators (plutonium-238) whose power drops every year: that is why the instruments are switched off one at a time. Its signals, with a power of about 20 watts, reach the Earth after almost a day of travel.

## History

It was launched on 5 September 1977, sixteen days after its twin Voyager 2, but on a faster trajectory: that is why it is called "1". On 14 February 1990, at Carl Sagan's suggestion, it turned around to photograph the Earth from about 6 billion km away: this was the "Pale Blue Dot", the Earth as a speck of light in a sunbeam. In February 1998 it overtook Pioneer 10 as the most distant human-made object. Between the end of 2023 and April 2024 a fault in the onboard computer interrupted its science data: the engineers fixed it by reprogramming it from 24 billion km away.

## Fun facts

- Around November 2026 Voyager 1 reaches for the first time a distance of one light day from the Earth, about 25.9 billion km. In the Atlas the light time is updated live.
- On board is the Golden Record: sounds and images of the Earth, music from all over the world and greetings in 55 languages, meant for whoever finds it.
- The onboard computer has a memory of a few tens of kilobytes.
