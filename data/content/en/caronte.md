## Science

Charon is Pluto's largest moon, with a diameter of 1,212 km, more than half that of the dwarf planet: in proportion it is the largest satellite in the Solar System. Pluto and Charon both rotate synchronously, always showing each other the same face, like two dancers holding hands.

The surface is made of water ice, marked by a canyon system longer than the Grand Canyon. At the north pole there is a reddish spot, Mordor Macula: according to the New Horizons scientists it forms when methane escaped from Pluto's atmosphere freezes onto Charon's cold pole and is turned by ultraviolet light into red organic compounds (tholins).

## History

In 1978 James Christy, at the U.S. Naval Observatory in Flagstaff, noticed that a "bulge" periodically appeared in photos of Pluto: it was a moon. The discovery finally made it possible to measure Pluto's mass, which turned out much smaller than expected. New Horizons photographed its details in July 2015.

## Fun facts

- From half of Pluto's surface Charon is always visible, standing still at the same point in the sky; from the other half it is never seen.
- The name comes from the ferryman of souls in Hades, but Christy also chose it because it recalled his wife's name, Charlene.
- The "Mordor spot" is an example of one world "painting" another: Charon's color comes partly from Pluto.
