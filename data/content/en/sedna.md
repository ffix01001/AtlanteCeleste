## Science

Sedna follows one of the most extreme orbits known: at its closest point to the Sun it comes to about 76 times the Earth–Sun distance, well beyond Neptune, and at its farthest it goes out to about 900 times. A full orbit takes about 11,000 years. It will reach perihelion around 2076, after which it will disappear back into the dark for millennia.

With an estimated diameter of between 900 and 1,000 km it is a possible dwarf planet, although not yet officially recognized. It is one of the reddest objects in the Solar System, colored by organic compounds transformed by radiation.

## History

It was discovered on 14 November 2003 by Mike Brown, Chad Trujillo and David Rabinowitz. The name comes from the Inuit goddess of the sea, who according to myth lives in the icy depths of the Arctic Ocean.

## Fun facts

- Sedna's orbit cannot have been shaped by Neptune, which is too far away. Among the hypotheses: a star that passed near the Sun when the Solar System was young, or a still unknown, very distant planet (the so-called "Planet Nine").
- From the surface of Sedna, at the farthest point of its orbit, the Sun would look like a very bright star, but not like a disk.
- Its perihelion passage in 2076 is a unique opportunity for a mission: the next one will come only in about 11,000 years.
