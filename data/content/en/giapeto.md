## Science

Iapetus has two faces: one hemisphere is as dark as coal, the other as white as snow. The difference comes from the dark dust collected as the moon moves forward along its orbit, which makes the nearby ice sublimate; the ice then settles on the colder, brighter areas, enhancing the contrast.

Along its equator runs a ridge up to 20 km high, which gives Iapetus the look of a walnut. Its orbit is tilted by about 15° with respect to the rings: from here the view of Saturn would be spectacular.

## History

Giovanni Domenico Cassini discovered it on 25 October 1671 and immediately noticed something strange: it was visible on one side of its orbit but disappeared on the other. He correctly deduced that one hemisphere had to be much darker than the other.

## Fun facts

- In Arthur C. Clarke's novel *2001: A Space Odyssey*, the "gateway" to the stars is on Iapetus (in the film it was moved near Jupiter).
- The equatorial ridge still has no agreed explanation.
