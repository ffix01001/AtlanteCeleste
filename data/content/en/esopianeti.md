## Science

An exoplanet is a planet that orbits a star other than the Sun. More than six thousand are known, and almost all of them were discovered without seeing them: from the slight wobble of the star, pulled back and forth by the planet (radial velocity method), or from the small drop in light when the planet passes in front of it (transit method).

The first method gives the planet's mass, the second its radius. When both are known we know its density, and so we can tell whether it is a world of rock like the Earth, a gas giant like Jupiter, or something that does not exist in the Solar System, such as «super-Earths» and «mini-Neptunes».

## How they are drawn here

The data (period, mass, radius, temperature, year and method of discovery) come from the NASA Exoplanet Archive: all the known systems within about 33 light years of the Sun, plus some of the most famous ones.

- The **star** is in its real position, with its color and its size.
- The **planet** goes around with the measured period, at the right distance from the star. The orbit is drawn circular and in the plane of the ecliptic: its real orientation in space is almost never known.
- The **surface** is only indicative: nobody has ever seen what an exoplanet looks like. The color only tells what type it is (rocky, ice giant, gas giant).

## History

The first planets outside the Solar System were found in 1992 around a pulsar, PSR B1257+12: unlikely worlds, around the remains of an exploded star. In 1995 Michel Mayor and Didier Queloz discovered 51 Pegasi b, the first around a Sun-like star: a gas giant so close to its star that it completes an orbit in four days. The discovery earned them the Nobel Prize in Physics in 2019.

## Fun facts

- The planet closest to us is Proxima Centauri b, a little more than four light years away: even with the fastest probe ever launched it would take thousands of years to get there.
- Around the small star TRAPPIST-1 orbit seven planets roughly the size of the Earth, all closer to their star than Mercury is to the Sun.
