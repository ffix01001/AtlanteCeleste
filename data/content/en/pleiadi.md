## Science

The Pleiades (M45) are an open cluster of young stars in the constellation Taurus, about 444 light years away (136 parsecs, a distance measured by the Gaia satellite). They contain more than a thousand stars, born together about 100 million years ago: hot, bright blue giants dominate and give the cluster its color.

Long-exposure photographs show a delicate blue haze around the brightest stars. It is not what remains of the cloud they were born from: it is an unrelated dust cloud that the cluster is passing through, reflecting the blue light of the stars.

## History

The Pleiades are mentioned by Homer in the Iliad and the Odyssey and by Hesiod, who used them as a calendar for the start of the harvest and of the sailing season. The Nebra sky disk, the oldest depiction of the sky that has come down to us (Bronze Age), shows them as a small group of seven dots.

In 1610 Galileo, in the *Sidereus Nuncius*, drew 36 of their stars instead of the 6 or 7 visible to the naked eye: one of the first proofs that the telescope revealed a universe richer than anyone could imagine.

## Fun facts

- In many cultures they are called the "Seven Sisters"; in Japan *Subaru*, the name (and the logo) of the car maker.
- To the naked eye you usually count six; with a very dark sky and sharp eyesight even more than ten.
- Their distance was long a puzzle: the measurements of the Hipparcos satellite (1990s) gave about 120 parsecs, disagreeing with other methods; Gaia confirmed the larger value, around 136 parsecs.
