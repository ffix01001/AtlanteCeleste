## Science

Haumea is the dwarf planet with the strangest shape: it spins in less than 4 hours, one of the fastest rotations among the large bodies of the Solar System, and this speed has stretched it like a rugby ball, about twice as long as it is wide.

Its surface is covered with crystalline water ice, and in 2017, watching Haumea pass in front of a star, astronomers discovered that it has a ring: the first found around an object beyond Neptune. It has two moons, Hiʻiaka and Namaka, and a "family" of icy bodies with similar orbits, probably fragments of an ancient collision.

## History

Its discovery was at the center of a controversy. Mike Brown's team had spotted it at the end of 2004 without announcing it; in July 2005 a Spanish team led by José Luis Ortiz Moreno announced it first, after having looked up the American team's observing logs online. The IAU did not officially credit the discovery to anyone, but listed the Spanish Sierra Nevada observatory as the location, while the Hawaiian name was proposed by Brown's team.

## Fun facts

- Haumea is the Hawaiian goddess of fertility and childbirth: the moons are named after two of her daughters.
- On Haumea there is a dark red spot richer in minerals and organic compounds, whose origin is still unknown.
- A "day" on Haumea lasts less than four hours: a sunrise every couple of hours.
