## Science

The James Webb Space Telescope is the largest and most powerful telescope ever launched. Its primary mirror, 6.5 meters across, is made of 18 hexagonal beryllium segments coated with gold. It observes mainly in the infrared, where the light of the first galaxies arrives, stretched by the expansion of the universe, and where the chemical fingerprints of the atmospheres of planets around other stars can be read.

To see the faint heat of the universe it has to be extremely cold: a sunshield as big as a tennis court, made of five very thin layers, keeps the instruments at about −230 °C. It orbits around the L2 point, 1.5 million km from the Earth, on the side opposite the Sun.

## History

It was launched on 25 December 2021 on an Ariane 5 rocket from Kourou, in French Guiana, as a joint mission of NASA, ESA and the Canadian Space Agency. The first images were presented in July 2022. Among its discoveries: galaxies formed less than 300 million years after the Big Bang and the first secure detection of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere of an extrasolar planet.

## Fun facts

- The launch was so precise that the telescope has fuel to stay operational well beyond the planned 10 years, perhaps 20.
- In the Atlas the James Webb is tied to the Earth: you can see its orbit around the L2 point.
