## Science

Mars is a rocky planet about half the size of the Earth, with a very thin atmosphere of carbon dioxide: the pressure at the ground is less than 1% of the Earth's. Its red color comes from iron oxides, a kind of rust that covers dust and rocks.

It hosts the most spectacular landforms in the Solar System: the volcano Olympus Mons rises about 22 km above the Martian reference level, and the Valles Marineris canyon system is more than 4,000 km long and up to 7 km deep. The polar caps contain water ice and dry ice. River valleys, deltas and sediments show that billions of years ago liquid water flowed there: that is why Mars is the main place to look for traces of past life.

The Martian day, called a "sol", lasts 24 hours and 39 minutes, and an axial tilt similar to ours gives it seasons, twice as long as Earth's.

## History

In 1877 Giovanni Virginio Schiaparelli, director of the Brera observatory in Milan, drew a detailed map of Mars with a network of "canali" (channels). In the English translation they became *canals*, that is artificial works, and the American Percival Lowell built the legend of the engineering Martians on that misunderstanding.

Mariner 4 (1965) sent the first close-up photos, Viking 1 and 2 (1976) made the first successful landings. Since then ever more capable rovers have followed: Sojourner, Spirit, Opportunity (active for more than 14 years), Curiosity and Perseverance, with the Ingenuity helicopter, as well as China's Zhurong. In orbit, the European probe Mars Express carries the MARSIS radar, developed in Italy, which in 2018 reported possible bodies of salty water under the south pole; the interpretation is still debated.

## Fun facts

- Sunsets on Mars are blue: the very fine dust scatters blue light around the Sun.
- On 19 April 2021 Ingenuity made the first powered flight on another planet. In the end there were 72 flights.
- Dust storms can envelop the whole planet for weeks: one of them, in 2018, ended the Opportunity mission.
- Gravity is 38% of the Earth's: a jump here would be almost three times higher.
- A few hundred meteorites that came from Mars have been found on Earth, thrown into space by ancient impacts.
- The moon Phobos is getting closer to Mars by almost two meters per century: in a few tens of millions of years it will be torn apart by tides or crash.
