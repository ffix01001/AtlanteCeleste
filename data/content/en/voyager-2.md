## Science

Voyager 2 is the only probe to have visited Uranus (January 1986) and Neptune (August 1989), after its flybys of Jupiter (1979) and Saturn (1981). Its "Grand Tour" was made possible by an alignment of the outer planets that occurs only about every 175 years, using the gravity of each planet as a slingshot towards the next. On 5 November 2018 it too entered interstellar space.

## History

It was launched on 20 August 1977, before Voyager 1. In August 2023 a wrong command tilted its antenna by 2 degrees and contact was lost: after two weeks NASA managed to "shout" a command to it with the full power of the Australian antenna in Canberra, and Voyager 2 turned back towards the Earth.

## Fun facts

- Everything we know up close about Uranus and Neptune, their moons and their rings comes from this one probe.
- Voyager 2 also carries a copy of the Golden Record.
- Since 2020 the only antenna able to send it commands is the one in Canberra, because Voyager 2 lies far to the south in the sky.
