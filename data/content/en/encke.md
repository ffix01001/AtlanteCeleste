## Science

Comet Encke has one of the shortest periods among known comets: it returns to perihelion every 3.3 years, coming closer to the Sun than Mercury. Its nucleus, about 5 km across, has lost most of its surface ices and is not very active today. The debris left along its orbit feeds the Taurid meteor shower, visible in autumn.

## History

It was first observed by Pierre Méchain in 1786, but it was the German astronomer Johann Franz Encke, in 1819, who showed that it was always the same object and calculated its orbit. It was the second periodic comet to be recognized, after Halley's.

## Fun facts

- In 2007 the STEREO probe photographed Encke's tail being torn away by a coronal mass ejection from the Sun, and then growing back.
- It is one of the most frequently observed comets in history: more than sixty recorded passages.
