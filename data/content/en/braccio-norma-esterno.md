## Science

The Norma-Outer Arm is a single, very long spiral arm that wraps around almost the whole Galaxy. Its inner stretch, the Norma Arm, passes near the end of the central bar, less than 15,000 light years from the center. After circling the Galaxy it reappears outside the Sun's orbit as the Outer Arm, about 40,000 light years from the center, in the direction of the winter constellations.

The outer stretch is poor in stars and rich in hydrogen: it marks one of the zones where the disk of the Galaxy begins to fade and to bend (the disk of the Milky Way is slightly "warped", like the brim of a hat).

## History

The name Norma comes from the small southern constellation of the Carpenter's Square (in Latin *Norma*), in whose direction the inner stretch is seen. The connection between the inner and the outer stretch as parts of a single arm emerged from maps of hydrogen and carbon monoxide, and from maser parallaxes.

## Fun facts

- Looking towards the constellation Auriga or Gemini in winter, the light of the most distant regions of the Outer Arm has traveled for about 13,000 years before reaching us.
- In the Atlante Celeste model the observed stretches follow the parameters of Reid and collaborators (2019); the connection on the hidden side of the Galaxy is an extension.
