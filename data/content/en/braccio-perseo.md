## Science

The Perseus Arm is one of the four great spiral arms of the Milky Way. It passes outside the Sun's orbit, about 6,000 light years from us in the direction of the constellations Perseus and Cassiopeia, and wraps around a good part of the Galaxy.

It contains some of the best-studied star-forming regions of the northern hemisphere and spectacular clusters, such as the Double Cluster in Perseus (h and χ Persei), visible to the naked eye as a milky smudge. Cassiopeia A, the remnant of a supernova that exploded about 350 years ago, also lies in this arm.

## History

In 2006 the distance of a star-forming region in the Perseus Arm (W3OH) was measured for the first time with the trigonometric parallax method, by observing a maser with the VLBA radio interferometer. It turned out to be about half of what had been estimated with indirect methods based on the rotation of the Galaxy: it was the proof that "kinematic" distances can be badly wrong, and it started the modern maps of the arms.

## Fun facts

- The Double Cluster is about 7,500 light years away: the light you see from it tonight set out when the first farming villages were appearing on Earth.
- In the Atlante Celeste model the observed stretch of the arm follows the parameters of Reid and collaborators (2019); the part beyond the galactic center is an extension.
