## Science

Comet Swift-Tuttle is the "mother" of the Perseids, the August shooting stars known in Italy as the Tears of Saint Lawrence. Every year, around 12 August, the Earth crosses the trail of dust that the comet has left along its orbit.

Its nucleus, about 26 km across, is huge for a comet: Swift-Tuttle is the largest known object that repeatedly passes close to the Earth's orbit. It returns to perihelion about every 133 years.

## History

It was discovered in July 1862 by Lewis Swift and, a few days later, by Horace Tuttle. In 1866 the Italian astronomer Giovanni Virginio Schiaparelli showed that the Perseids follow the same orbit as the comet: it was the first proof of the link between comets and shooting stars.

## Fun facts

- Its last perihelion passage was in December 1992; the next will be in July 2126.
- The grains of the Perseids enter the atmosphere at about 59 km/s: they burn up more than 100 km high.
