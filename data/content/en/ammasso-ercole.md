## Science

M13 is the brightest globular cluster in the northern sky: a sphere of hundreds of thousands of old stars, about 145 light years across, some 24,000 light years from us in the constellation Hercules. Its stars are about 12 billion years old: they are among the oldest in the Galaxy, born when the Milky Way was young.

Like all globular clusters, M13 orbits the center of the Galaxy along a path that takes it out of the disk, into the halo.

## History

It was discovered by Edmond Halley in 1714, who described it as a small smudge visible to the naked eye on clear nights. Charles Messier included it in his catalog in 1764.

## Fun facts

- On 16 November 1974 the Arecibo radio telescope sent the "Arecibo message" towards M13: 1679 radio pulses that, arranged in a grid, draw numbers, DNA, a human figure, the Solar System and the telescope itself. It will arrive in about 25,000 years, but by then the cluster will have moved.
- From dark skies it can be seen with the naked eye, between the stars eta and zeta Herculis; with a small telescope you begin to make out individual stars at the edges.
