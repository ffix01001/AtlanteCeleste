## Science

Comet 67P/Churyumov-Gerasimenko is the best-studied cometary body of all. It is shaped like a "rubber duck": two lobes joined by a narrow neck, probably two bodies that gently merged in the early stages of the Solar System. The nucleus is very porous, with a density about half that of water.

The Rosetta probe found organic molecules there, the amino acid glycine, phosphorus and molecular oxygen, as well as water with an isotopic composition different from that of Earth's oceans.

## History

The comet was discovered in 1969 by Klim Churyumov on photographic plates taken by Svetlana Gerasimenko at Alma-Ata (today Almaty). The Rosetta mission (ESA) reached it in August 2014 and accompanied it for two years around the Sun. On 12 November 2014 the Philae lander made the first landing on a comet: it bounced and ended up in a shadowed spot, and was only found again in photos in September 2016. On 30 September 2016 Rosetta also ended its mission by touching down on the nucleus.

## Fun facts

- Italy played an important role: the VIRTIS spectrometer, the GIADA dust analyzer and Philae's drill were Italian.
- Rosetta is named after the Rosetta Stone, and Philae after an obelisk from the island of Philae: both helped decipher hieroglyphs, just as the mission was meant to "decipher" comets.
