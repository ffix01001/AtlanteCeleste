## Science

Triton is Neptune's largest moon (2,700 km) and the only large moon in the Solar System that orbits in the direction opposite to its planet's rotation. It is almost certainly a Kuiper Belt object, similar to Pluto, captured by Neptune.

At about −235 °C it has one of the coldest surfaces ever measured, covered with nitrogen ice. In 1989 Voyager 2 discovered geysers there that shoot nitrogen and dark dust up to 8 km high, and an area of closely packed bumps that recalls the skin of a cantaloupe.

## History

William Lassell discovered it on 10 October 1846, just 17 days after the discovery of Neptune.

## Fun facts

- Because of its retrograde orbit, tides are slowly drawing it closer to Neptune: in a few billion years it will be shattered and might form a spectacular ring.
- Triton is larger than Pluto, which after all is a "relative" of its that stayed free.
