## Science

The Scutum-Centaurus Arm is one of the two most massive arms of the Milky Way. It starts near one end of the central bar, passes about 10,000 light years from the Sun in the direction of Scutum and Centaurus, then wraps around the opposite side of the Galaxy.

It is a place of intense star formation: along its innermost stretch lie huge molecular clouds and some of the most massive young star clusters in the Galaxy. In its outermost part, on the other side of the galactic center, it takes the name "Outer Scutum-Centaurus Arm".

## History

In 2008 the Spitzer Space Telescope, counting millions of stars in the infrared, indicated that Scutum-Centaurus and the Perseus Arm are the two arms richest in old stars: for a while people talked of a Milky Way with "two main arms". In 2011 Thomas Dame and Patrick Thaddeus discovered its most distant extension, beyond the galactic center, by observing carbon monoxide.

## Fun facts

- Seen from the Sun, the zone where this arm leaves the bar lies in the direction of the constellation Scutum, one of the smallest in the sky.
- In the Atlante Celeste model the observed stretch follows the parameters of Reid and collaborators (2019).
