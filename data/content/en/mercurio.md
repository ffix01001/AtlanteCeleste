## Science

Mercury is the smallest planet and the closest to the Sun: it completes an orbit in just 88 days. It rotates slowly and in a peculiar way, called a 3:2 resonance: it makes three rotations every two orbits. The result is that a solar day (from one noon to the next) lasts 176 Earth days, that is two Mercurian "years".

Without an atmosphere to hold the heat, its temperature range is the most extreme among the planets: from about 430 °C on the sunlit side to −170 °C at night. And yet in the polar craters, where the Sun never reaches, the MESSENGER probe confirmed the presence of water ice.

Mercury has an enormous metallic core, filling about 85% of its radius, and a weak global magnetic field. Its orbit slowly "rotates": a small advance of the perihelion, 43 arcseconds per century, which Newton's mechanics could not explain and which in 1915 became one of the first confirmations of Einstein's general relativity.

## History

Known since antiquity, it is hard to observe because in the sky it never strays far from the Sun. In 1965 radar showed that it rotates in about 59 days; it was Giuseppe "Bepi" Colombo, a mathematician and engineer at the University of Padua, who realized that this value is exactly two thirds of the orbital period. Colombo also suggested the trajectory that allowed the Mariner 10 probe to fly past Mercury three times (1974–1975), using the "gravitational slingshot" of Venus.

MESSENGER (NASA) studied it from orbit from 2011 to 2015. The European-Japanese mission BepiColombo, named after the Paduan scientist, is on its way.

## Fun facts

- On average it is Mercury, not Venus, that is the planet closest to the Earth, and to every other planet too: circling near the Sun, it never gets too far from any of them.
- Venus is hotter than Mercury, even though it is almost twice as far from the Sun.
- From some points on the surface, near perihelion, the Sun would seem to stop in the sky, go back for a while and then resume its course.
- As it cooled, the planet contracted: its radius shrank by a few kilometers, leaving long cliffs on the surface.
- The Caloris basin, about 1,550 km across, was created by an impact so violent that it upset the ground on the opposite side of the planet.
