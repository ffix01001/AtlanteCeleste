## Science

Io is the most volcanically active world in the Solar System: it hosts more than 400 active volcanoes, with sulfur plumes rising up to hundreds of kilometers and lava lakes such as Loki Patera. The engine of this activity is tidal heating: Io is in orbital resonance with Europa and Ganymede (when Ganymede completes one orbit, Europa completes two and Io four), and Jupiter's varying pull continuously deforms it, heating its interior.

Sulfur compounds color the surface yellow, orange, red and white. Every second Io loses about a ton of material, which forms a ring of charged gas around Jupiter (the plasma torus) and feeds the planet's auroras.

## History

Galileo discovered it in January 1610. In 1676 Ole Rømer noticed that the eclipses of Io came early or late depending on the distance between Earth and Jupiter, and deduced that light has a finite speed. In March 1979 the engineer Linda Morabito, analyzing a Voyager 1 image, discovered the first extraterrestrial volcanic plume; a few days earlier three scientists had predicted exactly that volcanism by calculating the tidal heat. Between 2023 and 2024 the Juno probe passed about 1,500 km from the surface.

## Fun facts

- Io is the densest body among Jupiter's moons and the only one practically without water.
- Io's lavas are probably hotter than present-day lavas on Earth.
- Io is crossed by an electric current of millions of amperes that connects it to Jupiter and leaves a bright footprint in the planet's auroras.
