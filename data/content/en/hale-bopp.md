## Science

Hale-Bopp was the "Great Comet" of 1997, one of the most observed ever. Its nucleus is exceptionally large, with an estimated diameter between 40 and 60 km, and that is why the comet was already very active when it was beyond the orbit of Jupiter. Besides the usual dust and gas tails, it showed a third one, of sodium, never seen before.

## History

It was discovered on the night of 23 July 1995, independently, by Alan Hale in New Mexico and by Thomas Bopp in Arizona, when it was still about 7 times the Earth–Sun distance away. It remained visible to the naked eye for about 18 months, a record. Its previous passage dates back about 4,200 years.

## Fun facts

- Thomas Bopp did not own a telescope: he discovered it with a friend's during an observing night in the desert.
- Its next return is expected in more than 2,000 years: a close encounter with Jupiter in 1996 shortened its period.
