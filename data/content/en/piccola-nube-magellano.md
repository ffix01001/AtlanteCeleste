## Science

The Small Magellanic Cloud is an irregular dwarf galaxy, a satellite of the Milky Way, about 200,000 light years away (62 kiloparsecs). It is elongated along our line of sight and poor in heavy elements: its stars and gas resemble those of the galaxies of the young Universe.

The two Magellanic Clouds are linked by the "Magellanic Bridge", a bridge of gas and stars, and they leave behind a very long tail of hydrogen, the Magellanic Stream, torn away by tidal forces and by passing through the hot halo of the Milky Way.

## History

It was precisely in the Small Cloud that the American astronomer **Henrietta Swan Leavitt**, working at Harvard Observatory, discovered in 1908-1912 the relation between the period and the luminosity of Cepheid variable stars. Since the stars of the Small Cloud are all at roughly the same distance, Leavitt could compare them directly: her discovery became the yardstick for measuring the distances of galaxies and allowed Hubble to discover that the Universe is expanding.

## Fun facts

- Next to the Small Cloud in the sky you can see the globular cluster 47 Tucanae, one of the finest in the sky: it is, however, much closer, inside our own Galaxy.
- It is not visible from Italy (declination about −73°).
