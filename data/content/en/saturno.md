## Science

Saturn is the second largest planet and the only one with an average density lower than water's (0.69 g/cm³). Like Jupiter it is made mostly of hydrogen and helium, with winds exceeding 1,800 km/h. At the north pole a hexagon-shaped jet stream, about 30,000 km across, has been stable for decades.

Its rings are the most spectacular in the Solar System: the main ones span about 280,000 km in diameter but are usually only about ten meters to a kilometer thick. They are made almost entirely of water ice, in pieces ranging from dust grains to blocks as big as houses. Small "shepherd" moons shape them, opening gaps and keeping thin rings such as the F ring compact.

Saturn has the largest number of known moons: 274 officially recognized in 2025. Among them are Titan, larger than the planet Mercury, and Enceladus, which hides an ocean beneath its ice.

## History

In 1610 Galileo saw Saturn with two strange "ears" and thought of three bodies close together; two years later, with the rings seen edge-on, the ears had disappeared. In 1659 Christiaan Huygens understood that it was a thin, flat ring, and in 1675 Giovanni Domenico Cassini discovered its main gap, which bears his name.

Pioneer 11 (1979) and the two Voyagers (1980–1981) flew past it. The Cassini-Huygens mission (NASA, ESA and the Italian Space Agency) studied it from orbit from 2004 to 2017: among other things, Italy built the probe's large main antenna. On 14 January 2005 the Huygens probe landed on Titan. On 15 September 2017 Cassini dived into Saturn's atmosphere, so as not to risk contaminating its moons.

## Fun facts

- Placed in a large enough bathtub, Saturn would float.
- Every 13–16 years the rings are seen edge-on from Earth and almost disappear in the telescope: the last time was in March 2025.
- The length of Saturn's day (10 hours, 33 minutes and 38 seconds) was measured in 2019 by studying the waves that the planet's vibrations imprint on the rings.
- According to Cassini's measurements the rings might be relatively young, perhaps a few hundred million years old, and they are slowly "raining" onto the planet. Their age is still debated.
- Saturn also has a huge, almost invisible ring, linked to the moon Phoebe, discovered in 2009 with the Spitzer infrared telescope.
