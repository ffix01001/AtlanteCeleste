## Science

The Eagle Nebula (M16), in the constellation Serpens, is a star-forming region about 5,700 light years away, in the Sagittarius-Carina Arm. Inside it is a young cluster of massive stars (NGC 6611) whose ultraviolet radiation and winds are sculpting and evaporating the cloud they were born from.

The columns of gas and dust that resist this erosion are the famous "Pillars of Creation": the tallest are about 4-5 light years long. At their tips, protected by the dust, new stars are still forming.

## History

The cluster was discovered by Jean-Philippe Loys de Chéseaux in 1745-46; Charles Messier cataloged it in 1764, also noticing the nebulosity. The photograph of the Pillars taken by the Hubble Space Telescope in 1995 has become one of the most famous scientific images ever; Hubble photographed them again in 2014 and the James Webb telescope in 2022, in the infrared, showing the stars hidden inside.

## Fun facts

- The name "Eagle" comes from the shape of the nebula in photographs, which recalls an eagle with spread wings: not from the constellation Aquila, which is nearby but different.
- You often read that the Pillars have already been destroyed by a supernova and that the news "has not reached us yet": it is a debated, unconfirmed hypothesis. The most recent observations suggest that they will slowly wear away over a few million years.
