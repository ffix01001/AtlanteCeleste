## Science

Uranus is an "ice giant": beneath an atmosphere of hydrogen, helium and methane (which gives it its cyan color) it hides a mantle of compressed water, ammonia and methane. Its strangest feature is its rotation axis, tilted by almost 98°: Uranus spins practically "lying down", rolling along its orbit. Each pole thus lives about 42 years of continuous daylight followed by 42 years of darkness.

It has the coldest planetary atmosphere in the Solar System, with lows around −224 °C, and it gives off almost no internal heat, unlike the other giants. Its magnetic field is tilted by about 59° with respect to the axis and does not pass through the center of the planet. Around it there are 13 thin, dark rings.

## History

On 13 March 1781 William Herschel, a musician and amateur astronomer, noticed from his garden in Bath a "star" that was moving. It was the first planet discovered with a telescope, and it suddenly doubled the known size of the Solar System. Herschel wanted to call it *Georgium Sidus*, "George's star", in honor of King George III; the mythological name proposed by Johann Bode prevailed.

The rings were discovered in 1977, by watching how a star "blinked" as it passed behind them. The only probe to have visited it is Voyager 2, on 24 January 1986. In 2024 a study showed that in those days the solar wind was exceptionally strong, so the magnetosphere seen by Voyager 2 was not very representative: one more reason to go back with a dedicated mission.

## Fun facts

- Before Herschel it had already been seen several times, but mistaken for a star: John Flamsteed cataloged it in 1690 as "34 Tauri".
- Its moons are named after characters by Shakespeare and Alexander Pope: Titania, Oberon, Miranda, Ariel, Umbriel...
- A year on Uranus lasts 84 Earth years: since its discovery it has completed fewer than three orbits.
- On the darkest, clearest nights it is just visible to the naked eye, if you know exactly where to look.
- A 2024 study reconstructed the true colors of Uranus and Neptune: they are more similar to each other than the famous Voyager images suggested.
