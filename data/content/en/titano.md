## Science

Titan is the second largest moon in the Solar System (5,150 km, more than Mercury) and the only one with a dense atmosphere: it is made mostly of nitrogen and presses on the ground with 1.5 times the Earth's pressure. An orange haze of organic compounds hides the surface.

Beneath that veil lies a strangely familiar world: rivers, lakes and seas, but full of liquid methane and ethane, fed by real rain. The largest seas, Kraken Mare and Ligeia Mare, lie near the north pole. Dunes of organic grains cover the equatorial regions, and under the ice crust there is probably an ocean of salt water. At −180 °C, Titan's chemistry resembles in some ways that of the early Earth.

## History

Discovered by Christiaan Huygens on 25 March 1655. On 14 January 2005 the European probe Huygens, carried by Cassini, descended by parachute through Titan's atmosphere and landed on a damp plain covered with ice pebbles: it is still the landing farthest from Earth ever achieved. NASA is preparing Dragonfly, a rotorcraft drone that will fly between sites on Titan.

## Fun facts

- With low gravity and dense air, on Titan a person with wings strapped to their arms could fly by flapping them.
- Titan's surface can only be "seen" in the infrared or with radar: Cassini mapped it through the haze.
- Kraken Mare is larger than the Caspian Sea.
