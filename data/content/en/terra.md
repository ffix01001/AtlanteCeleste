## Science

The Earth is the only known planet that hosts life and stable liquid water on its surface, which covers 71% of it. It is the densest planet in the Solar System (5.5 g/cm³) and the only one with true plate tectonics: the crust is divided into plates that move a few centimeters a year, raise mountains and recycle carbon.

The outer core of molten iron, churning, generates the magnetic field that deflects the solar wind and lights up the auroras. The atmosphere is 78% nitrogen and 21% oxygen, the latter produced over billions of years by photosynthesis. The axis, tilted by 23.4° with respect to the orbit, is the cause of the seasons.

## History

Around 240 BC Eratosthenes of Cyrene measured the Earth's circumference by comparing the Sun's shadows at Syene and at Alexandria in Egypt, obtaining a value surprisingly close to the real one. In 1543 Nicolaus Copernicus moved the Earth away from the center of the universe, and a century later Galileo gathered the evidence with his telescope.

The first photo of the Earth from space was taken in 1946 by a V-2 rocket launched in New Mexico. Images that became symbols followed: "Earthrise", the Earth rising over the lunar horizon (Apollo 8, Christmas 1968), the "Blue Marble" (Apollo 17, 1972) and the "Pale Blue Dot", a speck of light photographed by Voyager 1 from 6 billion km away (1990).

## Fun facts

- Days are getting longer: tidal friction slows the rotation by about 2 thousandths of a second per century. In the time of the dinosaurs a year had about 370 days.
- We are closest to the Sun at the beginning of January, in the middle of the northern winter: seasons depend on the tilt of the axis, not on the distance.
- The Earth is slightly flattened at the poles: the equatorial diameter exceeds the polar one by about 43 km.
- The point farthest from the center of the Earth is not the summit of Everest but that of Chimborazo, in Ecuador, thanks to the equatorial bulge.
- Every day the Earth collects tens of tons of dust and small meteorites.
- In the Atlas the Earth is oriented according to the official IAU model: the point lit at noon is the real one, at this very instant.
