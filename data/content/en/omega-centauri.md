## Science

Omega Centauri (NGC 5139) is the largest and most massive globular cluster of the Milky Way: a sphere of about 10 million old stars, with a mass of about 4 million solar masses, some 17,700 light years from us in the constellation Centaurus.

Unlike other globular clusters, its stars do not all have the same age and chemical composition. That is why many astronomers think Omega Centauri is the surviving core of a dwarf galaxy captured and torn apart by the Milky Way billions of years ago. In 2024, by measuring the motion of very fast stars at the center of the cluster with the Hubble Space Telescope, a team led by Maximilian Häberle found evidence of an "intermediate-mass" black hole of at least 8,200 solar masses.

## History

Ptolemy cataloged it as a star, and that is why it carries a Greek letter like stars do: the designation "omega" was given to it by Johann Bayer in 1603. Edmond Halley, observing it from the island of Saint Helena in 1677, was the first to describe it as a nebula; John Herschel, in the 1830s, recognized it as a globular cluster.

## Fun facts

- To the naked eye it looks like a fuzzy fourth-magnitude star.
- From Italy it can be seen only from the far south, very low on the horizon on spring nights: from southern Sicily it rises just a few degrees.
- In the heart of the cluster the stars are so crowded that the average distance between two stars is a fraction of a light year: a planet there would have a night sky with hundreds of stars brighter than Sirius.
