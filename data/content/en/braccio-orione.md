## Science

The Orion Arm, also called the Local Arm or Orion Spur, is the stretch of spiral where the Sun lives. It is shorter and less rich than the great arms of the Galaxy, but it is not a mere "bridge": the parallax measurements of recent years describe it as a segment several thousand light years long, full of gas and young stars, running between the Perseus Arm (farther out) and the Sagittarius-Carina Arm (farther in).

The Sun lies near its inner edge. Many of the wonders of the winter and summer sky belong to the Orion Arm: the Orion Nebula and the great associations of young stars in Orion, the Cygnus region with the Cygnus Rift, the Veil and North America nebulae.

## History

The name comes from the constellation Orion, in whose direction many of its star-forming regions lie. For decades it was considered a secondary spur; the maser parallaxes measured with radio telescope networks (VLBA and VERA) from the 2000s onwards showed that it is more extended and structured than was thought.

## Fun facts

- Almost all the stars we see with the naked eye belong to the Sun's neighborhood, that is to this arm.
- In the Atlante Celeste model the Local Arm is drawn fainter than the four main arms, as the observations show.
