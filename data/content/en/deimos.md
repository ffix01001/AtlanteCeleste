## Science

Deimos is the smaller and outer of the moons of Mars: about 15 × 12 × 11 km. Its surface looks smoother than Phobos's, because a thick layer of dust (regolith) fills the craters. It orbits about 23,500 km from the center of Mars in a little over 30 hours, just slower than the planet's rotation.

## History

Asaph Hall discovered it on 12 August 1877, six days before Phobos. In March 2025 the European probe Hera, on its way to the asteroid Didymos, passed near Mars and photographed Deimos, including the side that can never be seen from the planet.

## Fun facts

- Since it orbits at almost the same speed as Mars rotates, from a point on the surface Deimos stays visible for about two and a half days before setting.
- Seen from Mars it does not look like a disk but like a very bright star.
- Two of its craters are named Swift and Voltaire, in honor of the writers who "predicted" the Martian moons.
