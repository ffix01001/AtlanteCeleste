## Science

Enceladus is small (about 500 km) but it is one of the most interesting worlds in the Solar System. It is the most reflective body known, covered with fresh ice. At the south pole, from four fractures called "tiger stripes", come jets of water vapor and ice grains that feed Saturn's E ring.

Flying through these plumes, the Cassini probe found salt water, silica, organic molecules, molecular hydrogen and, in 2023, phosphates: all clues to an underground ocean in contact with a warm rocky seafloor. For many scientists it is the best place in the Solar System to look for life today.

## History

Discovered by William Herschel on 28 August 1789. The Voyagers showed a strangely young surface; the plumes were discovered by Cassini in 2005.

## Fun facts

- Cassini flew through the plumes several times, sometimes just 25 km above the surface, "tasting" the ocean without touching it.
- Part of the ice that Enceladus shoots into space falls back as snow onto its own surface.
- It reflects almost all the sunlight that strikes it: that is why it is also one of the coldest bodies in the area.
